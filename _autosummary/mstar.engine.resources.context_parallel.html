mstar.engine.resources.context_parallel#

Context parallelism: a paged KV pool’s token positions placed over a group of ranks that would otherwise hold the same KV heads.

Page ids stay logical and are the same on every rank, so allocation, prefix keys, offload and admission need nothing new. Within a page, logical slot s belongs to partner s % P and sits at its physical slot s // P, so each rank stores page_size // P slots a page. PagedKVConfig.place resolves the placement. A pool that keeps a sliding window places its first group of layers alone; the window’s stay whole on every partner (PagedKVConfig.placed_groups).

Attention over such a pool (ContextParallelWrapper) runs on each rank over its own slots and returns the partial with its log-sum-exp; the partners exchange partials and merge them, exactly.

A step split over SP partners by stored position (stripe_owners) attends by passing queries round the partners instead (RingAttentionWrapper): each rank attends every partner’s queries over its own slots, and the partials go back to their queries’ rank.

class mstar.engine.resources.context_parallel.RingAttentionWrapper(placement, page_size, dtype, paged, device)[source]#

Bases: object

A split step’s attention over an SP-placed pool by striped pass-Q. The step’s tokens are striped over the partners by KV position (stripe_owners), so each rank holds the queries whose K/V it writes into its own slots. In round i a rank attends partner (r - i) % P’s queries over its slots, the stored context and the step’s own tokens together, with one call (_ring_rows); the partial goes back to that partner, which merges it into its running result (merge_lse).

One causal call is exact because a partner’s queries in a row sit at KV positions p0 + aP, and this rank’s keys at or below p number (p - r) // P + 1: one more per query, so the kernel’s bottom-right causal mask over the local length to the row’s last query is the right one. Only a row’s first query can see no key here (p0 < r); it plans as a row of its own over one sink slot, masked out of the merge (_masked), and its own rank always holds a key of it. Not causal, each row’s queries see its whole length, and a row with no slot here is masked the same way. A row this partner holds no query of is left out of its plan.

paged is one eager kernel over this rank’s slots per partner, all planned per step. Eager only: the node never splits a captured step (SeqShardSpec.eager). The partner plans differ by rank and no admission bounds them: FlashInfer’s paged manager, the one backend that takes a placed pool (PlacedAttention), so the one that plans a ring, bounds no plan at admission, as no refusal of its planner on lengths is known. A backend whose planner may refuse on lengths (MLA’s FA planner) would have to bound every rank’s partner plans at admission before it could plan a ring, or one rank alone could refuse a lockstep step.

Every row keeps a length of at least 1 and a last page holding at least one slot, as on one rank: FlashInfer’s split-KV planner counts a row’s pages where its kernel counts its tokens, and a last page with none of this rank’s slots puts the two out of step.
