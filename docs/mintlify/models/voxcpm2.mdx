Tokenizer-free TTS: MiniCPM-4 base and residual LMs emit continuous latent patches through a local flow-matching DiT, decoded by AudioVAE V2 in streamed chunks. Zero-shot, voice design, and cloning from a clip. 48 kHz.

Registry key: `voxcpm2`. Checkpoint: `openbmb/VoxCPM2`.

## Deployment configs

The CLI maps the model keys to these deployment configs. See [Installation](/installation) for dependencies; model-specific requirements and limits follow on this page:

| CLI model key | Config in the repository |
|---------------|--------------------------|
| `voxcpm2`     | `configs/voxcpm2.yaml`   |

From the repository root, launch a deployment with:

``` bash
mstar serve voxcpm2
```

For client requests and response formats, see [Using a Server](/clients).

## Install and choose a voice mode

Install with `pip install -e '.[voxcpm2]'` and serve with `mstar serve voxcpm2 --gpus 0`. `/v1/audio/speech` takes the text alone (zero-shot), a voice description in `instructions` (voice design), a `ref_audio` clip (cloning) or `ref_audio` with its transcript in `ref_text` (continuation). Output is 48 kHz.

## Request types and ignored fields

`min_len`, `max_len` and `inference_timesteps` must be integers (a whole float such as `16.0` is accepted; `2.7` and `"20"` are 400s), `max_new_tokens` / `max_output_tokens` (160 ms patches) follow the server's integer rule, and `cfg_value` is a number in \[0, 10\]. `inference_timesteps` is a deployment setting a request may only repeat. A budget past the model's `max_len` is refused. `seed` fixes the diffusion noise. `voice` is dropped (VoxCPM2 has no named voices) and a sent `speed` is logged as ignored.

## Long text can end early

One request with long text can end early: on some seeds the stop head fires mid-text, as it does in the official `voxcpm` package (a 983-character Russian text stopped about three quarters of the way in on 5 of 10 seeds there). Split long text into several requests.

## Capacity and text limits

A request may hold the whole 8,192-position context, and nothing preempts a request once a KV cache is full, so each LM's cache defaults to 2,049 pages (32 such requests and the sink page, about 9.7 GB for both) and the YAML sets `max_concurrent_requests: 32`; the conductor queues the rest. Start-up refuses a cap the caches cannot hold, so raise `resources.base_kv` and `resources.res_kv` `max_num_pages` with it. Text over `max_text_tokens` (1,365 by default) gets a 400: English speech runs at up to about 2.9 patches per token, so longer text may not fit in the 4,096-patch (655 s) audio budget.

## Startup compilation cost

The Talker's decode step (both LMs, the local DiT solve, the stop head) is `torch.compile`'d and captured as CUDA graphs at start-up, so a first start-up with a cold Inductor cache takes 10 to 30 minutes (591 s on a B200, 1,723 s on an H100), and about two minutes once the cache is warm. `model_kwargs: {compile_talker: false}` in the deployment YAML captures the eager step instead: on a B200 the server is up in about 15 seconds, with about a third less throughput at concurrency 1 and a sixth less at concurrency 8.

## Talker prefill capture

The Talker's prefill (both LMs over the prompt, the first patch's solve and the stop head) is captured too, per total prompt tokens (up to 2,048) and prompts in a step (up to 32); a step past them runs the LMs eagerly.

## VAE worker placement

`configs/voxcpm2.yaml` runs the VAE decoder's windows on the worker's side lane (`side_stream: true`), beside the Talker's steps rather than taking turns with them.

## VAE compilation

The streaming VAE decoder is `torch.compile`'d at start-up, about a quarter faster per window batch; `model_kwargs: {compile_vae: false}` decodes eagerly.
