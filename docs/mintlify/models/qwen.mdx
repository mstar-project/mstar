## `qwen3_5_{0.8,2,4,9,27}b`

Hybrid-attention VLM (text, image and video in, text out): gated DeltaNet linear attention interleaved with full attention, plus a ViT tower.

Checkpoint: `Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B`.

## `qwen3_8_27b`

Qwen3.8-27B on the Qwen3.5 implementation (same architecture); ships a TP4 config.

Checkpoint: `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B`.

From the repository root, launch a deployment with:

``` bash
mstar serve qwen3_5_0.8b
```

For client requests and response formats, see [Using a Server](/clients).

Text, image and video chat on the Qwen3.5 dense family (five sizes; MoE variants are not supported yet). Served on both `POST /generate` and `/v1/chat/completions` (`image_url` and `video_url` parts). Images and videos may be interleaved with text anywhere in the prompt, and prefill follows the order they were written:

```text
mstar serve qwen3_5_4b --gpus 0
```

## Video input and capacity

Video follows transformers' `Qwen3VLProcessor` exactly: frames are decoded with torchcodec on the host (FFmpeg's shared libraries must be installed; without them a video request gets a 400, as there is no fallback decoder), sampled at the checkpoint's `video_preprocessor_config.json` rate (2 fps, 4 to 768 frames for the released checkpoints), resized within its pixel budget, and paired into temporal groups of two frames. Each group is preceded by a `<t seconds>` timestamp in the prompt and placed in MRoPE like a one-frame image. The prompt layout is the one transformers uses from 5.10 (and vLLM and SGLang): the template's `<|vision_start|>`/`<|vision_end|>` around a video stay, with each group's own pair inside.

Earlier transformers replaced the outer pair too; mstar keeps the 5.10 layout whatever version is installed. Resizing follows the installed transformers version: transformers 5.22 turns on the per-frame pixel cap of qwen-vl-utils by default, which lowers the token count of short clips. Notes:

- The vision encoder attends within each image and each video frame group separately, and its captured CUDA graphs hold up to 16384 patches and 64 such segments per prompt. A prompt over either limit runs the encoder without the graph (still compiled, so only the launches are extra): a long video at ordinary resolution exceeds the patches, and one over ~64 s with frames resized below ~256x256 pixels exceeds the segments.
- An encoder step packs the images and videos of up to 8 requests into one replay. Requests that fit alone but would overflow it together run one after another in that step. While the block loop is captured, a request too large for a replay alone (a long video) gets a step of its own. The packed GEMMs' shapes follow the step's total patches, so an image's embeddings can differ at the last bit with what it is batched beside.
- On two GPUs, `configs/qwen3_5_9b_dp2.yaml` (a whole replica per GPU) serves video traffic faster than `configs/qwen3_5_9b_tp2.yaml`, whose vision encoder runs on one rank while the other waits for it in the lockstep LLM: 9B on 2xH200 with TP-async on, DP2 gave 12.5-44% more output tok/s on 30 s and 60 s videos at c=16 and c=32.
- A long video is one wide prefill: one 1-3 minute video is ~11-12k tokens, beyond the largest captured `prefill_vision` bucket (4096), so it runs eagerly.
- Size the KV pool for video. `configs/qwen3_5_*.yaml` keep the default 2048 pages of 128 tokens (262k tokens per deployment); 32 concurrent long videos need about twice that. Measured on Qwen3.8-27B at TP4: 32 concurrent long videos on the default pool gave 67 tok/s with most requests timing out, and 1,585 tok/s with none failing on 8192 pages. Raise `resources: kv_cache: max_num_pages` to fit the concurrency you serve.
- Decoding happens in the API server process, before the request is queued, and counts against `MSTAR_MAX_VIDEO_PIXELS`: at the default, a 1080p video over about 259 s gets a 400.

## Recurrent-state capacity

Most layers are gated DeltaNet, so a request holds a recurrent-state slot as well as a KV allocation. Set `gdn_state.max_slots` in `configs/qwen3_5_*.yaml` to the concurrency you want plus one for the sink; CUDA-graph capture and padded replays use the sink, not slots. A slot is ~20 MiB for the 0.8B and ~50 MiB for the 27B at TP4 (half that in bf16), hence not the 256-slot default. The state defaults to the checkpoint's `mamba_ssm_dtype` (fp32 for the released checkpoints); `gdn_state.state_dtype: bfloat16` halves state traffic at some precision and needs FlashInfer's fused bf16 decode kernel (K = V = 128).

## Capture buckets and startup

Text prefill and mixed steps replay captured graphs by total tokens, on the ladder `32 ... 8192` (powers of two, plus 2176 as a mixed step's slack: a step passes its budget only when its decode rows leave its next prompt less than `min_chunk_tokens` of it, and then by at most its decode rows); a step pads to its bucket and a longer one runs eagerly. A text-prefill step, one with no decode rows beside it, also has 1152, 1280, 1536 and 1792, so a prompt a little past 1024 tokens doesn't pad to 2048; a mixed step captures only the ladder's powers of two and 2176. 4096 and 8192 stay in the default ladder for a deployment that raises `max_batch_tokens` or turns chunking off, which fills them exactly.

The ladder leaves out slack rungs above them (4352, 8704): such a deployment adds them to its own ladder, and without them a step just past a power of two pads to the next one and a step past 8192 runs eagerly. The serving config's `prefill_token_buckets` sets a deployment's own ladder per walk (see [Serving](/serving)): `configs/qwen3_5_9b*.yaml` take the powers of two to 2048 for `prefill_text` and `mixed`, and 1152-1792 for `prefill_text`, since the LLM's 2048-token step budget (`MAX_BATCH_TOKENS`) caps both and at the default `min_chunk_tokens` no step passes it.

Each bucket adds captures and startup time.

A captured decode step builds its rotary tables inside the graph from the planned positions, rather than staging tables built eagerly before the replay (that build sat on the per-step critical path: +13% output tok/s at c=32 on 0.8B, H200). Greedy output can differ from earlier releases at near-tie tokens; prefill and eager steps are unchanged (`knobs: {ingraph_decode_rope: true}` does the same for captured text prefill and mixed steps).

The prefill, mixed and vision captures compile in Inductor's deterministic mode whatever `-O` says: autotuning them bought no GPU time and took about a fifth of the cold start. The decode capture keeps the `-O` level's tuned kernels. `knobs: {compile_deterministic: true}` compiles it deterministically too: greedy output then reproduces across server processes and the cold start more than halves. One-row decode pays for that at the larger sizes (c=1 at 9B on B300: 270 against 286 tok/s; at 27B TP4 on H200: 151 against 169) and gains at 4B on B300 (372 against 353); from c=4 up it makes no difference.

## Sampling and reasoning

`temperature`, `top_p`, `max_tokens` and `seed` are the standard fields. `top_k`, `min_p`, `repetition_penalty`, `penalize_prompt` and `enable_thinking` (default true; the template opens a `<think>` block; `chat_template_kwargs.enable_thinking` also works) are read by the model but are not OpenAI fields — pass them via `extra_body`. Sampling defaults to the checkpoint's generation_config, under whatever the request sets. Qwen3.5-27B's sets temperature 0.6, top_k 20 and top_p 0.95, and Qwen3.8-27B's temperature 1, top_k 20 and top_p 0.95; the 0.8B, 2B, 4B and 9B ship none, so the engine sampler's defaults apply. A request's `min_p` above 0 is refused (a 400) unless the checkpoint sets a `min_p`, which turns the sampler's min-p filter on; none of these does. `max_output_tokens` may not exceed `max_position_embeddings`.

## Token IDs and log probabilities

With `resources: {sampler: {enable_logprobs: true}}` in the deployment's YAML, `"return_logprobs": true` in `model_kwargs` on `/generate` puts each token's raw log-prob in its chunk's `metadata` (`{"logprobs": [...]}`, one per token, as `token_ids`). Qwen3.5 reports them; other models refuse the request with a 400. Add `enable_processed_logprobs: true` and `"logprobs_mode": "processed"` for the log-prob under the distribution it was actually drawn from (penalties and top-k/top-p applied); greedy requests want `"raw"`, as a greedy token scores 0 there. Top alternatives and OpenAI's `logprobs` are not supported yet. On any deployment, `"return_token_ids": true` adds each text chunk's sampled ids (`{"token_ids": [...]}`), stop tokens included, so a trainer need not re-tokenize the text.

## Serve LoRA adapters

**LoRA adapters.** A `resources: lora:` block in the serving config serves per-request LoRA adapters (PEFT `adapter_model.safetensors`; the scaling comes from the `adapter_config.json` beside it). The OpenAI `model` field picks one, as in vLLM: the base model's name (or its HF repo id, or no `model` at all) runs the base model, an adapter's name runs that adapter, and any other name is a 400. `GET /v1/models` lists the adapters beside the base model. On `POST /generate`, pass `model_kwargs: {lora_adapter: <name>}`, checked the same way. Rows on different adapters, and on none, share a batch:

```text
resources:
  lora:
    max_slots: 4        # adapters resident on the GPU at once
    max_rank: 16        # the largest rank served; slots are sized to it
    cpu_capacity: 16    # adapters kept in pinned host memory
    adapters:
      sql: /adapters/qwen3_5-4b-sql/adapter_model.safetensors
      chat: /adapters/qwen3_5-4b-chat   # a PEFT directory names its adapter_model.safetensors
```

## Validate adapter targets

Every adapter the config names is checked at startup, before any worker starts (LoRA layers present, rank within `max_rank`, only the targets below, shapes that fit them), and one that fails stops the server with an error naming it.

Adapters may touch every module PEFT adapters for Qwen3.5 on the Hub touch:

- every linear of the text stack, as `target_modules="all-linear"` picks them: the full-attention layers' `q_proj` (its output gate included), `k_proj`, `v_proj` and `o_proj`, the gated DeltaNet layers' `linear_attn.in_proj_qkv` (or `in_proj_q` / `_k` / `_v` split, as Tinker trains them), `in_proj_z`, `in_proj_a`, `in_proj_b` and `out_proj`, and every layer's `gate_proj`, `up_proj` and `down_proj`;
- the LM head (`lm_head`, or Tinker's `unembed_tokens`) and the token embedding (PEFT's `lora_embedding_A` / `_B` on `embed_tokens`). With tied weights (0.8B, 2B, 4B) each delta stays on its own side, as PEFT applies them;
- the vision tower, as unsloth's vision fine-tunes adapt it: each block's `attn.qkv`, `attn.proj`, `mlp.linear_fc1` and `mlp.linear_fc2`, the merger's `linear_fc1` / `linear_fc2`, and the conv `patch_embed.proj`. One spec covers both nodes, sized by the one `lora:` block; the vision encoder node has a pool of its own (`lora@vision_encoder`, beside the LLM's `lora@LLM`) and runs the request's adapter too.

Module names may be in any layout PEFT writes for the model: the released checkpoint's (`model.language_model.layers.*`, `model.visual.*`), the text-only causal LM's (`model.layers.*`), the inner model's (`language_model.layers.*`) or the bare text model's (`layers.*`). An adapter on the MTP head is accepted and that part ignored (the head never runs). The DeltaNet in-projections are one fused GEMM, so their deltas are one shrink and one expand per layer, added before the conv reads the output.

Refused with a 400 that says why, when the adapter is named: DoRA (`use_dora`), `modules_to_save` (full copies of a module, e.g. a classifier `score` head or a retrained `lm_head`), trainable tokens, bias LoRA, other PEFT methods (OFT), a module outside the list above, a module whose shapes do not fit (an adapter for another size), and a linear-shaped LoRA on `embed_tokens` (`lora_A.weight` of `[r, hidden]`, as some Tinker exports name a tied head's LoRA; rename its keys to `lm_head` to serve it on the head).

## Adapter memory and capture

Every target is sized into each slot and runs its kernels on every step that has an adapter row, whether that row's adapter uses it or not. The LM head and embedding are vocabulary-sized: at `max_rank: 16` and 4 slots, about 40 MB each per GPU (the head's split over TP ranks, the embedding's whole on each). So with `runtime_updates` off (no adapter can arrive after startup), the pools keep only the targets the configured adapters touch, and a vision encoder whose adapters are all text-only builds no pool. With it on, every target is kept unless the YAML lists the ones to keep:

```text
resources:
  lora:
    targets: [qkv_proj, o_proj, in_proj_fused, out_proj, gate_up_proj, down_proj,
              qkv, attn_proj, mlp_linear_fc1, mlp_linear_fc2, merger_linear_fc1,
              merger_linear_fc2, patch_embed_proj]
```

An adapter deployment captures and compiles the LLM node as one without adapters does, each bucket twice: with the LoRA kernels, and base-only for steps none of whose rows names an adapter. The vision encoder's block loop is captured base-only; a step with an adapter row runs it uncaptured. The LoRA pool keeps no resident table yet, so a `lora:` block turns resident decode planning off on the LLM node (the server logs it): every decode step, base-only ones included, is planned on the host as before resident planning.

Every size takes the same `resources: lora:` block; `max_rank` must cover the largest adapter served. Example blocks (Hub adapters, ranks as published; no shipped config has one, so add the line to that size's serving config):

```text
# e.g. in configs/qwen3_5_0.8b.yaml: a vision fine-tune (blocks and merger, rank 16)
lora: {max_slots: 4, max_rank: 16, adapters: {agnews: <hammamwahab/jev-agnews-lora>/adapter_model.safetensors}}
# e.g. in configs/qwen3_5_2b.yaml: vision with the conv patch embed (rank 32)
lora: {max_slots: 4, max_rank: 32, adapters: {comma: <wjbmattingly/comma-qwen-3.5-2b>/adapter_model.safetensors}}
# e.g. in configs/qwen3_5_4b.yaml: LM head and embedding (rank 8), Tinker (rank 32)
lora: {max_slots: 4, max_rank: 32, adapters: {pirate: <William-Gao1/qwen3.5-4b-pirate-lora>/...,
                                              oneinbox: <dKhRr/oneinbox-qwen3.5-4b-lora>/...}}
# e.g. in configs/qwen3_5_9b.yaml or qwen3_5_9b_tp2.yaml: released layout (16), Tinker (32)
lora: {max_slots: 4, max_rank: 32, adapters: {nimble: <bespokelabs/Bespoke-Nimble-9B>/...,
                                              rlvr: <lucabaroni/qwen3.5-9b-rlvr-reward-hacking>/...}}
# e.g. in configs/qwen3_5_27b_tp4.yaml: text and vision (rank 16)
lora: {max_slots: 4, max_rank: 16, adapters: {tcg: <heftyTuna/qwen35-27b-tcg-lora>/...}}
```

Under TP the LLM's targets split as its linears do (the 27B's single K/V head per rank at TP4, and the DeltaNet `a` gate padded per rank, included); the vision encoder runs on one GPU in every shipped config.

Both frontends serve LoRA: a request's `model` names the adapter it runs under (`ServedModels`), and `/v1/models` lists the adapters beside the base model. Runtime updates (below) are the Python frontend's only for now: `mstar-server` reads the served adapters once at launch and has neither route, so `mstar serve` refuses `--rust-frontend` with `runtime_updates: true`.

## Load and unload adapters at runtime

Adapters can also be loaded and unloaded while the server runs, with vLLM's endpoints and bodies. They make the server read paths a client names, so they are off unless the `lora` block opts in, and answer 404 otherwise (as vLLM's do without `VLLM_ALLOW_RUNTIME_LORA_UPDATING`). The block may name no adapters at all (`resources: {lora: {max_slots: 4, runtime_updates: true}}`):

```text
curl -X POST localhost:8000/v1/load_lora_adapter -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
    -d '{"lora_name": "sql", "lora_path": "/adapters/qwen3_5-4b-sql"}'
curl -X POST localhost:8000/v1/unload_lora_adapter -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
    -d '{"lora_name": "sql"}'
```

`lora_path` is a PEFT directory (its `adapter_model.safetensors` is read) or a safetensors file, readable at the same path by the API server and every worker. A load is checked first (LoRA layers present, rank within `max_rank`, only the targets above, shapes that fit them), then read by every worker that hosts a node with a LoRA pool (the LLM's or the vision encoder's); the name is selectable, and listed by `/v1/models`, once all of them have it. A load that fails on any worker is undone on all of them and answered with a 400, as is a name that is already loaded.

A refusal says why the adapter cannot be served (its rank, its modules, its shapes); a file that cannot be read at all gets the same answer whether it is missing, unreadable or not an adapter, and the details go to the server log. A worker that does not answer within `runtime_update_timeout_s` (default 120) fails the load with a 504, and the load is undone everywhere, so the name is free to try again.

To change an adapter's weights, unload it and load it again: each adapter is pinned to the digest of the weights checked when it was configured or loaded, and a worker that reads other weights from its path (the file overwritten in place) refuses them, on every rank, rather than serve them under the old name and prefix-cache entries. An unload stops new requests on the name at once (they get the 400 an unknown adapter gets; an unknown name in the unload is a 404); if the conductor refuses it, the name is selectable again. Requests already running on it finish on its weights, after which its slot and host copy are freed; loading the name again before then is a 400 that says so.

## Qwen3.8 checkpoint behavior

Qwen3.8-27B (`qwen3_8_27b`) is the Qwen3.5 architecture and runs on the same code, served from `configs/qwen3_8_27b_tp4.yaml` (TP4):

```text
mstar serve qwen3_8_27b --gpus 0,1,2,3
```

Its `output_gate_type: swish` names the gated DeltaNet norm's activation, which is the SiLU every Qwen3.5 checkpoint already uses. transformers ignores the key and hard-codes SiLU. Video is still refused and the MTP head is skipped. The chat template's new `reasoning_effort` and `preserve_thinking` are not forwarded: every request gets the template's defaults (`xhigh` and preserved), whatever the client sends, and a request that sends one is logged as ignoring it.
